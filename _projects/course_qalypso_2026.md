<p class="ml-4"><strong>→ See my own lecture notes for the first part of the course <a href="{{ '/assets/pdf/courses/galicia-2026/4-Lecture-III.pdf' | relative_url }}">here (PDF)</a>.</strong></p>

This course explores how semidefinite programming (SDP) can be used to tackle quantum information problems that do not initially appear to be SDPs. We first examine how changes of variables and analytical reformulations turn problems involving measurement incompatibility and quantum channel discrimination into SDPs. We then consider approximation methods: seesaw algorithms for finding achievable strategies, and outer approximations and SDP hierarchies for establishing impossibility proofs. Case studies include channel discrimination without memory, the search for the most incompatible measurements, and entanglement detection through the hierarchy of symmetric extensions.
